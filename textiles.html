From the Workbench: Drafting the "Jumpa"

A hand-drafted apparel pattern from our studio — the front & back bodice, yoke join, and short sleeve for a jumper dress — kept here as a working reference and checked against standard pattern-cutting practice.

Tools on the Table

Pattern paper, a tailor’s pencil, a wing/protractor ruler for the shoulder slope, a French curve for the armhole and sleeve cap, hemming gum for holding facings, and a fabric measuring tape marked in both inches and centimetres — which is exactly why you’ll spot both units in the steps below.

1. Drafting the Front Bodice +
  1. Draw the 6 vertical lines to indicate the Shoulder Line, Chest Line, Stomach/Tommy Line , Vent Line, Dresslength Line & the Fold Line based on the Body measurement taken.
  2. Collar measurement ÷ 2, then divide that result by 3, minus 1/4″ — neck-width measurement.
  3. Add 1/2″ to the neck-width measurement for the neck-depth measurement.
  4. Curve the neckline inward, 1/2″ apart from the centre front and the shoulder line.
  5. Shoulder length ÷ 2 for the shoulder-width measurement.
  6. Mark 2″ inward and join it to the end of the neck point to form the shoulder line.
  7. Chest ÷ 4 for the horizontal chest measurement.
  8. Chest ÷ 4 + 2″ for the vertical chest measurement.
  9. From the 2″ inward mark on the shoulder line down to the chest line, divide the distance into 2 equal parts.
  10. From that midpoint, mark 2cm downward for the armhole line.
  11. From the top of the vertical chest line, mark 7″ down along the dress length for the stomach line.
  12. Mark the full dress length, then add 2″ for the fold.
  13. At your discretion, mark 2″ to 7″ up from the dress-length hem for the vent line.
  14. On the dress-length line, mark Chest ÷ 4 + 2″ + 1/2″ vertically.
  15. On the stomach line, mark Chest ÷ 4 + 2″ − 1/2″ vertically.
  16. Join the top chest-line mark to the stomach-line point, then continue to the top of the dress length.
  17. Draw a curve from the vent to meet the stomach line.

2. Drafting the Back Bodice +
  1. Place the front bodice on the fabric on fold.
  2. Measure 4″ from the front bodice neckline outward on the fabric (on fold).
  3. Mark 3″ outward from the front bodice shoulder line to draw a diagonal to the shoulder line, then cut a little beyond the front bodice.
  4. Measure 1/2″ inward on the shoulder line of the front bodice.
  5. Fold the measured 4″ on the back panel onto the 1/2″ inward mark on the front panel.
  6. Make a notch at the points where the back and front bodice meet.
  7. Mark 1cm upward from the midpoint between the shoulder line and the chest line, on the back bodice.
  8. From the notch, draw a curve through the shoulder-line end point of the front bodice, through the 1cm mark, to the chest-line end point — refreshing the armhole.
  9. Mark 1/2″ inward from the fold line on the back bodice.
  10. Draw a curve from the centre-back mark to meet the notch marked earlier.
  11. Cut along the curve, from the centre-back neck to the notch.

The front bodice paper sits on top; the back bodice paper is underneath.

How to read this photo: the red dotted line is the armhole after it's been raised by 1cm and redrawn — wider than the front armhole, to make room for the shoulder overlap. The black dashed curve at the neck is the back neck curve; the 4″ fold marked there becomes a 1/2″ notch once folded down. Where the two red lines meet at the shoulder is the notch — your matching point when you sew back to front.
3. Joining the Front Bodice to the Back & Yoke +
  1. Align the front panel and facing at the notch, and press.
  2. Measure 1/2″ along the neck curve and sew, back-stitching at the start and end.
  3. Notch along the joined seam, close to it, without cutting through.
  4. Press the bottom of the facing with hemming gum to hold it to the front panel.
  5. Press the front seam so the notched neck flips onto the facing.
  6. Turn up the facing to the back of the front panel and press.
  7. Sew the front neck along the joint, back-stitching start and finish.
  8. Align the front bodice onto the back panel, wrong side of the back bodice facing up, then place the wrong side of the front bodice on top — right sides facing on the back bodice pieces.
  9. Measure a 1″ fold at the bottom of the yoke and press with hemming gum.
  10. Place the right side of the yoke onto the right side of the front panel.
  11. Align the right shoulder from the armhole, not the neck — the left side takes the zip for a male garment, and vice versa for a female garment.
  12. Measure 1/2″ on the aligned right shoulder, pin, sew, and press.
  13. Mark 1/2″ on the yoke along the neck and draw a curve; flip the front panel out from under the yoke.
  14. Align and pin the yoke to the back bodice, then sew along the 1/2″ neck mark so it meets exactly the edge of the front panel at the right shoulder.

Pinning the facing to the front panel at the neckline.

How to read this photo: the small paper diagram (top left) shows the facing sitting directly behind the front panel, matched at the neckline notch. In the photo, both hands are pinning the facing piece to the front panel, right sides together, 1/2″ from the raw edge — this is steps 1–2 above. The yoke steps (8–17) aren't pictured here since they build on this same facing seam, just at the shoulder instead of the neck — the same “pin at 1/2″, sew, press” rhythm applies.
4. Cutting the Short Sleeve +
  1. Measure 6″ to get the bicep line.
  2. Secure a bi-fold (cross-fold) fabric and press.
  3. Measure Sleeve length + 1/2″ to get the hem line.
  4. Measure 2″ from the sleeve length for the fold.
  5. On the hem line, measure Arm Around ÷ 2 + 1″.
  6. Diagonally, from the centre front to the top of the bicep line, measure Armhole length − 1/2″; divide that into 3 equal parts.
  7. From the first (bottom) mark on the armhole length, mark 2cm outward.
  8. Use a French curve to join the second mark (from centre front) through to refresh the edge line.
  9. Join the second mark to the third mark inward, using the French curve.
  10. Join the top of the bicep line to the top of the hem line inward, with a slight curve, using the French curve.

5. Cutting the Fabric +
  1. Fold your fabric so the centre front and centre back both sit on the fold, as marked on the paper pattern.
  2. Lay the front bodice, back bodice, and sleeve pieces onto the fabric, matching each fold line to the fabric’s fold.
  3. Pin or weight the paper in place, then trace around each piece with tailor’s chalk or a fading fabric pen.
  4. Double-check every notch is marked on the fabric before you cut — these are your matching points at the shoulder, armhole, and neckline.
  5. Cut along your chalk lines, cutting a little wide of the neckline and armhole curves so you have room to true them up before sewing.

6. Joining the Short Sleeves to the Armholes +
  1. From the wrong side, align the notches on the short sleeve with those on the back panel, and pin on the left and right. Repeat on the other side — make sure it matches at both ends, and don’t forget the 1/2″ caught section that secures the front panel.
  2. Align the underarm (armpit) lines, the Arm Around endpoints, and the dress length, then pin — pinning along enough to hold the sides in place.
  3. Mark 1″ alternately at both sides, then stitch to complete the dress.
  4. Neaten all the seam allowances.

How This Compares to Standard Pattern-Cutting Practice

  • Neckline formula checks out. Halving the collar and dividing by 3 is mathematically the same as the classic “neck size ÷ 6” rule used in standard block-drafting texts for front neck width — the −1/4″ adjustment keeps it snug rather than gaping.
  • Armhole depth is within range. Chest ÷ 4 + 2″ sits inside the accepted band (roughly chest/bust ÷ 4 plus 1.5–2″) that standard blocks use for a semi-fitted garment.
  • No separate bust/waist dart. Fitted Western blocks (e.g. Aldrich-style) shape the waist and bust with darts; this draft is a direct, dartless cut — standard for loose-fitting garments like the jumpa, kaftan, and boubou styles common in West African tailoring. It trades close fit for drape and speed, by design rather than by omission.
  • Sleeve cap follows a recognised approach. Dividing the armhole length into three to place the cap curve points mirrors the AH÷3 logic used in formal sleeve-cap-ease calculations.
  • Where it differs from textbook notation: commercial patterns are usually drafted in one consistent unit and reference a printed measurement chart with abbreviations defined up front. This draft mixes inches and centimetres (e.g. the 2cm armhole and back-neck marks) — worth standardising to one unit for a repeatable, teachable version of the pattern.
